MyJAMS is developing interactive installation formats that let audiences explore rhythm, timing, and musical coordination through immersive and participatory experiences.

One example is our 100-metronome installation, which turns synchronisation into a visible and audible public experience. These installation ideas connect directly to the research behind ARME: how people coordinate in time, how groups adapt to one another, and how technology can make those processes tangible.

We are interested in partnerships with museums, festivals, schools, venues, and cultural organisations that want to create accessible music technology experiences for public audiences.
